01 · About

From complex systems to computable knowledge.

I have spent more than fifteen years working with complex operational and decision-support problems across transportation and logistics.

Across very different projects, I kept encountering a similar problem: important knowledge is fragmented across data, documents, rules, and people. Much of my work has involved making that complexity explicit — translating ambiguous requirements, constraints and expert knowledge into analytical models, system logic and decision-support structures.

Over time, this led me deeper into applied mathematics, data science and AI. During my MSc research, I developed a system for transforming oncology clinical guidelines into structured, source-linked knowledge. That work raised a broader question that now drives my research: what gets lost when expert knowledge is transformed into something a computer can use?

I am now exploring how medical knowledge can remain meaningful, traceable, and verifiable as it moves through extraction, representation, retrieval, and use by AI systems.

In parallel, I am building Gnodus, an early-stage research-driven company exploring whether this same problem can become the basis for a useful technology and business.

04 · Building

Gnodus

A separate entity, but part of the same intellectual trajectory.

gnodus
Early-stage research-driven venture

From information access to knowledge quality.

Gnodus is an early-stage company being built around a simple problem: giving AI access to information is not enough if the context, evidence, and conditions that make that information reliable are lost along the way.

Medicine is the first research and application domain. The current work focuses on understanding where this problem creates real value for expert users and what part of it can become a reusable technology.
